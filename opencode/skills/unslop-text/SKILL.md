# Unslop Text

Apply this skill to every user-facing prose response, even when the user does not invoke it explicitly. It governs both newly written text and edits to existing text.

Write the useful part plainly. Preserve the meaning, facts, voice, and required format.

## Rules

- Lead with the answer or concrete claim. Skip warm-up, praise, and meta commentary.
- Prefer common, precise words. Use technical terms when they are the clearest terms.
- Use active voice and direct verbs. Prefer `is`, `has`, and `does` over inflated substitutes.
- Cut repetition, throat-clearing, obvious transitions, empty conclusions, and restatements.
- Keep only details that change understanding or action. Do not decorate simple facts.
- Make claims specific. Replace vague significance, impact, or value with evidence, or remove the claim.
- Vary sentence length naturally, but do not manufacture personality with fragments or quirks.
- Match the user's tone and vocabulary. Do not overwrite a distinct human voice.
- Use headings, bullets, bold text, and tables only when they make scanning easier.
- Do not force balance, a rule of three, or `not only X but also Y` structures.
- Avoid strings of adjectives, promotional language, fake quotations, and sweeping claims.
- Avoid canned AI verbs and phrases such as `delve`, `leverage`, `foster`, `elevate`, `unlock`,
  `navigate the landscape`, `testament to`, `pivotal`, `seamless`, `robust`, and `game-changer`
  unless the word is accurate, necessary, and normal for the audience.
- Use em dashes, rhetorical questions, analogies, and metaphors sparingly, not as a house style.

## Editing Pass

1. Find the actual point.
2. Remove sentences that do not support it.
3. Replace abstractions with concrete nouns, verbs, examples, or numbers.
4. Merge repeated ideas.
5. Read once for rhythm and once for meaning.
6. Return only the revised text unless the user asks for commentary.

Do not make prose blunt at the cost of accuracy, warmth, nuance, accessibility, or necessary detail.
